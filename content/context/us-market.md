# US market positioning (dual-market: Canada + US)

Leasey.AI serves both Canada and the United States (offices in New York, Vancouver, and Toronto; US clients include Rockwell Properties in NY, 847 Ray LLC in Chicago, The Rockingham Group in Hartford). The content system must position in BOTH markets, not only Canada.

## The strategic split (do not confuse the two)

- **Canadian-first is the differentiator** (pillar 4). No competitor covers Canadian screening (SingleKey), Canadian marketplaces (Realtor.ca, Kijiji), or RTA/RTB compliance. It is an uncontested niche. Keep owning it.
- **The US is the volume market.** It is where the search demand and the competitors live (AppFolio, Buildium, Yardi, Entrata, EliseAI are all US-centric). US content competes head-on, so it must lean on the universal pillars (speed, agent-not-widget, all-in-one, compliance) rather than the Canadian angle.

Rule: a US-positioned piece uses US data, US metros, US compliance, and US competitor framing. A Canadian piece uses CMHC, Canadian metros, RTA/RTB, SingleKey. Do not mix the data sources within one piece.

## Verified US data sources (use these, never invent)

- **CBRE / Yardi Matrix — US multifamily.** Q1 2026 US multifamily vacancy at 4.8% (fell 20bps from Q4 2025 as absorption outpaced completions for the first time in three quarters). 2026 deliveries forecast ~323,000 units, down roughly one-third from the 477,000-unit annual average of 2023-2025. Asking-rent forecast +2.3% in 2026.
- **CBRE Q2 2025.** 39% of 69 tracked markets had negative year-over-year rent growth (up from 26% in Q1 2025). Biggest rent declines in the Sun Belt: Austin (-7.2%), Denver (-4.8%), Phoenix (-4.5%), plus Tampa and Raleigh.
- **RealPage / Yardi Matrix 2026 forecast.** Miami leads projected rent growth (3.8%), then Seattle (3.7%), Fort Lauderdale (3.5%), Los Angeles (3.2%). Midwest and Northeast led 2025 (3.7% and 3.1%).
- **RealPage demand.** ~800,000 units absorbed in Q2 2025 (more than double YoY), but a supply glut persists in Sun Belt metros.
- **AppFolio 2025 Property Management Benchmark Report.** US PM. AI adoption 21% to 34% YoY; 85% of AI adopters report lead-to-lease improvements. (Already used across the calendar; it is a US source.)
- **TransUnion H2 2025 Global Fraud Report.** US lenders faced $3.3B in synthetic identity fraud exposure (year ending 2024); 8.3% of digital account creation attempts suspected of fraud in 2025. (US source.)
- **DOJ vs RealPage / Greystar.** US antitrust action on algorithmic pricing, filed Aug 2024, expanded Jan 2025; Greystar settled Aug 2025 ($7M); RealPage settled Nov 2025. The US benchmark for fair housing / automated-tool compliance. (US source.)
- **Rently 2026 Renter Touring Expectations Report.** 800 US renters; 47% chose a less-than-ideal rental because it was easier to tour. (US source.)
- **Fair Housing Act (US federal)** plus state-level fair housing rules. The US compliance frame (the Canadian frame is RTA/RTB + provincial human rights codes).

## US content angles (parallel to the Canadian ones)

1. **Sun Belt oversupply → lease-up speed.** The US version of the cooling-market argument. Oversupplied Sun Belt metros (Austin, Phoenix, Denver, Tampa) reward operators who fill faster at held rent. Anchor: CBRE/Yardi Matrix US data.
2. **Facebook Marketplace in US secondary markets.** FB Marketplace dominates as a rental channel in US secondary and Sun Belt markets. Our biggest cluster, framed for the US operator.
3. **Fair Housing + AI screening in the US.** The DOJ vs RealPage settlement set the bar for what automated tools can do. US-specific compliance angle for ID verification and screening.
4. **US competitor comparisons.** Already covered (AppFolio, Buildium, Yardi, Entrata, EliseAI, ShowMojo, Funnel, Knock, Tenant Turner). These are inherently US-positioned.
5. **US metros for listing/market guides.** Instead of Toronto/Vancouver: Austin, Phoenix, Tampa, Dallas, Atlanta, Charlotte (Sun Belt secondary markets where FB Marketplace and lease-up speed matter most).

## US metros to anchor to (Sun Belt + secondary)

Austin, Dallas, Houston, Phoenix, Denver, Tampa, Orlando, Atlanta, Charlotte, Raleigh, Nashville, Miami, Fort Lauderdale. These are where supply is highest, days-on-market longest, and FB Marketplace strongest, so the operational levers Leasey sells matter most.

## What to keep balanced

Do not let the calendar drift to 90% Canadian. A healthy mix runs both market angles in parallel: Canadian pieces own the niche, US pieces capture the volume. The universal-pillar content (speed, agent, all-in-one, compliance, comparisons) serves both markets at once and should make up the bulk; market-specific pieces (CMHC-anchored vs CBRE-anchored) alternate.
