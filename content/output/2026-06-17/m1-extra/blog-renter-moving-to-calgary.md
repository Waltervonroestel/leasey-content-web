# Moving to Calgary in 2026: rents, neighbourhoods, and what every newcomer should know

Calgary has become one of Canada's most-watched rental markets, for reasons both good and frustrating depending on who you ask. If you are moving here this year, here is what you actually need to know.

## Rent ranges (early 2026)

Median monthly rent for a one-bedroom: approximately $1,750 to $1,900 [VERIFICAR with Rentals.ca and CMHC Q1 2026 report].
Median monthly rent for a two-bedroom: approximately $2,200 to $2,400 [VERIFICAR].

These ranges shift by neighbourhood. Inner-city Beltline and Bridgeland trend above the median. The northeast (Falconridge, Saddleridge) and parts of the deep south trend below. Vacancy in the city is around 7.3% as of Q1 2026, per Yardi, the highest among major Canadian markets. That means bargaining power has shifted a little toward renters this year. Use it.

## Five neighbourhoods to actually consider

1. **Beltline.** Walkable, dense, close to downtown. Most expensive but the trade-off is you can live without a car if you want. Best for young professionals.
2. **Bridgeland.** Pre-war character, hilltop views, short walk or bike to downtown. Quieter than Beltline. Newer condos mixed with character buildings.
3. **Inglewood and Ramsay.** Inglewood is the original neighbourhood of Calgary, slightly farther from downtown but with a real music and food scene. Ramsay just east is the cheaper cousin.
4. **Kensington.** West side, river-adjacent, walkable, parks, well-served by transit. Family-friendlier than Beltline.
5. **Mission and Cliff Bungalow.** River-adjacent on the south side, walkable to 17th Avenue, generally quieter and more residential than Beltline.

## What every newcomer underestimates

- **Cost of car ownership.** Auto insurance in Alberta is among the highest in Canada. If you are coming from BC or Ontario, double-check your premiums before you commit to a car-dependent neighbourhood.
- **The chinook winds.** Calgary gets warm, dry winds in winter that can move the temperature 20 degrees in a few hours. Pleasant, but they wreak havoc on headaches and dry skin. Worth knowing.
- **Distance is measured in time, not kilometres.** A "20-minute drive" can become 50 in rush hour. Listings will quote drive times to downtown that do not reflect rush. Check Google Maps at 8am, not Sunday afternoon.

## What to ask your landlord before you sign

1. Is the unit covered by Alberta's standard residential tenancy agreement? Some short-term rental setups are not.
2. What utilities are included? Water often is. Electricity rarely.
3. Is there secure parking, and is it included or extra?
4. What is the snow-clearing arrangement for the building?
5. Pet policies, deposit amount, and conditions for the deposit refund.
