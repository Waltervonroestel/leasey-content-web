# AI in leasing and Fair Housing: what to ask your vendor before going live

If a tenant sues your operation in 2026 over a screening decision made by AI, what does your audit trail look like?

The question is not theoretical. Per Multifamily Dive's reporting on the DOJ vs RealPage case (2025), Greystar was added as a co-defendant. Harbor Group is dealing with separate scrutiny over voucher holder screening. The legal layer around AI in leasing is moving fast, and most operators are signing AI vendor contracts written by the vendor's lawyers, not theirs.

This is a practical checklist for what to ask your vendor before you scale automation across your screening or pricing decisions.

## What to ask

1. **Which decisions does the system make on its own, and which does a human sign off on?** The right answer for any Fair Housing-adjacent decision (eligibility, screening verdict, pricing of a specific application) is a human. If the vendor cannot draw that line, that is the conversation.
2. **What gets logged, by whom, and for how long?** A defensible audit trail names the model, the inputs, the version, the human reviewer, and the date. Anything less is the operator wearing the legal exposure.
3. **How does the system handle voucher holders, ESA requests, and disability accommodations?** These are the categories that show up first in Fair Housing complaints. The system should treat them as exception flows, not as edge cases.
4. **What does the contract say about indemnification?** Most AI vendor contracts put the compliance burden on the property manager. Negotiate this before you scale.

## What to verify

- That the vendor's screening logic is documented, not "proprietary black box."
- That the platform stores, on your behalf, the data your jurisdiction requires for the period it requires.
- That an actual human at the vendor can speak to the screening decision if a regulator or a plaintiff's lawyer requests it.

## What to document on your side

Even with the best vendor, the operator carries the standard of care. A defensible posture includes:

- A written policy describing where AI is used in your leasing funnel and where it is not.
- Training records for the team members who review the AI's flags.
- A periodic audit of decisions the AI surfaced and how the team handled them. Quarterly is reasonable. Monthly during a roll-out.
