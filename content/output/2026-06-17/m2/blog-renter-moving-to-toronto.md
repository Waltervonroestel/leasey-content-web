# Moving to Toronto in 2026: rents, neighbourhoods, and what every newcomer should know

Toronto's rental market has finally cooled, but the city is still expensive enough that the difference between a smart neighbourhood pick and a wrong one is a thousand dollars a month. Here is what you need to know if you are moving here this year.

## Rent ranges (early 2026)

Median monthly rent for a one-bedroom in Toronto: approximately $2,300 to $2,600 [VERIFICAR with Rentals.ca, urbanation, and CMHC Q1 2026 reports].
Median for a two-bedroom: approximately $3,000 to $3,400 [VERIFICAR].

These are city-wide medians. East Toronto and Etobicoke trend below. Downtown and midtown trend above. Vacancy across the GTA is around 2.5 to 3% [VERIFICAR with Yardi Q1 2026], much tighter than Calgary or Edmonton. Bargaining power is still mostly with landlords, but less so than in 2023.

## Five neighbourhoods to actually consider

1. **Leslieville and Riverside.** East-end, walkable, streetcar-served, family-friendly, with rents 10 to 15% below downtown for similar quality.
2. **The Junction and Junction Triangle.** West-end, formerly industrial, now full of cafes and breweries. UP Express to Pearson runs through it.
3. **Liberty Village.** Dense, condo-heavy, walkable to downtown. Best for young professionals who do not mind sharing the sidewalk on Friday nights.
4. **St. Lawrence.** Closest to downtown for renters who want walkable transit and the historic St. Lawrence Market. Older buildings, sometimes higher utility costs.
5. **East York.** Family-oriented, leafy, served by Line 2 subway. Rents notably below downtown for two-bedrooms.

## What every newcomer underestimates

- **TTC delays.** Subway shutdowns on weekends for maintenance are routine. If you commute downtown, check the TTC service alert map before committing to a neighbourhood far from a Line 1 or Line 2 station.
- **Winter slush at intersections.** A January thaw and refreeze creates ankle-deep slush ponds at downtown crosswalks. Waterproof boots are not optional from December to March.
- **Hydro vs gas heat.** Toronto rentals vary a lot. A high-rise condo with hydro-only heat can run $250 a month in January. Ask before signing.
- **Locker, parking, gym fees.** Toronto condo buildings often list these as amenity fees separate from rent. Confirm the total before you compare two listings.

## What to ask your landlord before you sign

1. Are you covered by the Ontario Residential Tenancies Act? Most rentals are, but some sub-rentals and certain unit types are exempt.
2. What utilities are included? Hydro is the most variable.
3. Is the locker, parking spot, or balcony included or extra?
4. What is the building's pet policy?
5. What is the rent control status? (Buildings first occupied after November 15, 2018 are exempt from Ontario rent control.)
