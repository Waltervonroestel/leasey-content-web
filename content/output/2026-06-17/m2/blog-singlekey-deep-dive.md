# SingleKey integration: Canadian tenant screening inside the leasing funnel

If your Canadian leasing team is still pasting tenant data from screening reports into your CRM by hand, you are running a 2018 workflow in a 2026 market.

Per Yardi's Q1 2026 Multifamily Report, Canadian national vacancy is at 5.1%, with Calgary at 7.3% and Edmonton at 6.2%. The market is rewarding speed and consistency at the screening step. Anything that adds latency between "application submitted" and "screening verdict" is costing leases.

This is the gap the SingleKey integration inside Leasey.AI closes. Both companies announced the partnership last month. Here is what it does inside an operator's workflow.

## What changes for the leasing team

When an applicant submits an application inside Leasey, the data flows into SingleKey automatically. No manual re-entry. Credit, identity, eviction history, and Canadian-specific tenant data run as part of the same workflow that captured the inquiry and booked the showing.

For TEREZ, the 154-unit lease-up in Cochrane, Alberta, this means the screening step now lives inside the leasing dashboard instead of in a separate browser tab. The team sees the verdict, the underlying data, and the next-step recommendation in one view. The lease-up cadence stays predictable.

## Why Canadian-built screening matters

A US-built screening platform doing a Canadian credit check is doing a translation. Canadian credit bureaus, ID norms, employment verification practices, and tenancy history are not 1:1 with the US system. Mid-market Canadian operators have historically duct-taped this by running a US screening tool and then manually layering Canadian context. The work is real, and the error rate compounds.

SingleKey was built Canadian-first. So is Leasey. The integration removes the translation layer.

## What this does not do

The screening verdict is not auto-applied to a decision. The Leasey screening layer (including [Discrepancy AI](/features/liza/) for inconsistency flags and biometric ID verification) is structured so the team sees the data, sees the flags, and decides. Every Fair Housing-adjacent decision has a human attached.

That structure matters for compliance teams answering to provincial tenancy boards. The audit trail tells them what the system saw, what the human did, and when.

## How it fits the Canadian-first stack

The Canadian-first posture has been the long position at Leasey.AI from the beginning. Kijiji syndication. Duuo by Co-operators for tenant insurance. Now SingleKey for screening. Each integration is selected because it is the option Canadian operators actually use.

For Canadian mid-market operators evaluating their leasing stack this year, the question to put on the table is: how many tools in your current screening flow are US-first with a Canadian configuration toggle?
