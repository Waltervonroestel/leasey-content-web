Talking to a couple of operators last month, the question we kept circling was not "should we use AI in leasing." It was "what happens when we get sued for using AI in leasing."

Per Multifamily Dive's reporting on the DOJ vs RealPage case (2025), Greystar was added as a co-defendant. Harbor Group is dealing with separate scrutiny over voucher holder screening. The legal layer is not theoretical anymore.

My standing answer when this comes up: the risk lives in one specific place. AI that makes decisions a court will later say should have been made by a human. Screening, eligibility, pricing. Anything that materially affects whether a person gets housed.

The position we took at Leasey.AI when designing this layer is straightforward. The agent handles inquiries and tour booking. The screening flags inconsistencies and surfaces them. The human signs off on every decision that has Fair Housing implications. Liza never auto-denies an application.

I came from QuadReal and the institutional Canadian side before this. The compliance teams I worked with care more about audit trail than about elegant automation. The platform that wins enterprise is the one that gives them both.

If your AI vendor cannot tell you which decisions humans still own, that is the conversation to have before scaling.
