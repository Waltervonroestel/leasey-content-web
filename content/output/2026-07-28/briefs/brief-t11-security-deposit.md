## Addition 1 · The downloadable template (spec, not the finished file)

Two files, same content, same field order: **`leasey-itemized-security-deposit-deduction-letter.pdf`** (fillable form fields) and **`leasey-itemized-security-deposit-deduction-letter.docx`** (editable, with grey placeholder text).

**Do not write the full template prose in this brief.** This is the build spec for whoever produces it.

**Block 1 · Letterhead**
Landlord or management company legal name · mailing address · phone · email · date the letter is issued.

**Block 2 · Tenant and tenancy identifiers**
Tenant full legal name(s) · forwarding address · address of the rented unit including unit number · lease start date · lease end date · **date the tenant surrendered possession** (this is the date most state clocks run from, and it is the field landlords most often leave blank).

**Block 3 · Deposit accounting header**
Original security deposit amount held · any interest accrued (label the row "Interest, if required by state law") · **total held**.

**Block 4 · Itemised deduction table.** The core of the document. Repeating rows, minimum 8 rows plus an "add rows" note.

| Column | Content | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| Item / location | "Bedroom 2 carpet", "Kitchen cabinet door" | Vague labels are what lose disputes |
| Description of damage or charge | Plain description of the condition | Several states require precise detail |
| Repair or remedy performed | What was actually done | Required where the landlord does the work themselves |
| Vendor or self-performed | Name of contractor, or "self-performed" | Self-performed work triggers extra disclosure in California |
| Labour hours and hourly rate | Only completed when self-performed | Directly required by Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.5 |
| Amount | Currency figure | |
| Receipt / invoice attached (Y/N) | Tick box | Drives the attachment checklist in Block 7 |

**Block 5 · Totals**
Total deductions · **balance returned to tenant** (or **balance owed by tenant**, as a separate clearly labelled row, never merged with the refund row).

**Block 6 · Refund method and timing**
Cheque or electronic transfer · payee name · date sent · address sent to.

**Block 7 · Attachments checklist**
Receipts and invoices · estimates where work is not yet completed · move-in and move-out condition reports · dated photographs. One tick box per line.

**Block 8 · Signature and delivery record**
Landlord or agent signature and printed name · date · delivery method used · tracking or certified mail number.

**Block 9 · State-law footer**
A single line reading: "State law governs the deadline and the contents of this statement. Confirm your state's requirement before sending." Followed by an inline reference back to the deadline table on this page. **No state-specific legal advice inside the file.** The file is jurisdiction-neutral by design, which is also what keeps it usable in all 50 states and in Canada.

**Gating:** ungated. This page's whole problem is that it withholds the thing it promises. Putting the file behind a form recreates the gap. The download block carries the demo CTA next to it instead.

## Addition 3 · New H2 "What Can a Landlord Deduct From a Security Deposit"

Exact-match on the highest-volume, worst-positioned keyword on the page (pos 96, vol 110). Two lists, plainly labelled, no hedging.

**H3: Deductions landlords can generally make**
Unpaid rent · unpaid utility bills the tenant was responsible for under the lease · damage beyond normal wear and tear · cleaning required to return the unit to its move-in condition · removal and disposal of abandoned property · unreturned keys, fobs, or remotes · early termination charges where the lease provides for them and state law permits them.

**H3: Deductions landlords generally cannot make**
Normal wear and tear (faded paint, minor carpet flattening in traffic lanes, small nail holes, worn finishes from ordinary use) · repairs of pre-existing damage documented at move-in · routine repainting between tenancies where state law treats it as wear and tear · upgrades and improvements that betterment the unit rather than restore it · charges with no itemisation or supporting evidence.

**H3: Where the line sits: normal wear and tear versus damage**
The practical test, in two sentences: wear and tear is what time and ordinary use do to a unit that was lived in properly, damage is what a specific act or neglect did to it. The evidence that decides it is the move-in condition report compared against the move-out condition report, both dated and both signed.

Bold: **normal wear and tear**, **move-in condition report**.

This section carries the keyword `security deposit deductions list` (pos 19) as well, which is why it sits directly above the existing "Common Reasons for Security Deposit Deductions" H2 rather than replacing it. The existing H2 stays exactly as published.

## Figures to correct (Rule D)

**1. "$126" California threshold: WRONG. Correct it to $125.**
California Civil Code § 1950.5(g)(2) sets the threshold at **$125**, not $126. Where deductions exceed $125, the landlord must attach copies of the invoices or receipts to the itemized statement. Change the figure and add the citation.
Source: [Cal. Civ. Code § 1950.5](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-1950-5/)

**2. "$5-8 per letter" for certified mail: OUT OF DATE. Replace with current USPS pricing, or delete.**
As of the July 2026 USPS rates the Certified Mail fee alone is **$5.55**, before postage. Adding Return Receipt costs a further **$2.91** electronically or **$4.65** for the physical green card. A one ounce Certified Mail letter with electronic Return Receipt therefore runs to roughly **$9**, and closer to **$11** with the green card. The published "$5-8 per letter" understates the real cost of the delivery method the page recommends. **Recommended fix:** replace with "from $5.55 for Certified Mail, rising to roughly $9 to $11 once postage and Return Receipt are added (USPS, July 2026)", cited. If the writer will not carry a dated price, delete the figure entirely and describe the method without a number. Do not leave the old range.
Source: [USPS Certified Mail rates, 2026](https://www.certifiedmaillabels.com/usps-postal-rates)

**3. "14 to 60 days after lease termination": KEEP, now sourced.** The range is accurate against the statutes in the table above: 14 days at the fast end (New York, Vermont) and up to 60 days at the slow end (Colorado, where a lease may extend the period to a 60-day maximum). Attach the deadline table as its evidence and cite Vermont and Colorado inline as the two ends of the range.

**4. Deposit caps of "one to three months' rent": DELETE. Decided, not flagged.** No single statute supports a nationwide range, and caps have moved recently in several states. The sentence comes off the page and **no per-state cap column is added to the table**: that column would double the verification work on a table that already carries eight states, each needing its own statute link, and it is not what the focus keyword is about. The page is about the deduction letter, not about how much a landlord may collect up front. Walter decided this on 28 July.

No `[VERIFY]` markers remain in this brief. Every figure above is either corrected with a live source, or explicitly marked for deletion with the reason written out.
