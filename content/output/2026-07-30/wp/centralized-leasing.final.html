Centralized Leasing: What It Is, Who It Works For, and How to Get It Right

Centralized leasing is a model where one team handles leasing for multiple properties from a single hub, replacing the traditional setup of on-site agents at each building. For multifamily operators managing portfolios across several locations, it cuts redundant roles, speeds up response times, and creates a consistent renter experience across every property.

The term “central leasing” is often used interchangeably with “centralized leasing.” Both describe the same operational shift: moving leasing activity away from individual properties and into a shared team that serves the entire portfolio.

Why operators are centralising now

The economics of on-site leasing are getting harder to justify. According to the 2025 NMHC 50 Report, the 50 largest apartment managers in the U.S. now oversee 24% of the nation’s apartment stock, with Greystar alone managing over 946,000 units. At that scale, the pressure to reduce per-unit operating costs pushes operators toward shared-services models. Centralisation is one of the highest-impact moves available because leasing is both labour-intensive and repetitive across properties.

At the NAA Apartmentalize 2024 session “Democratizing Centralization for Large and Small Firms”, panellists from Bozzuto, Veritas Investments, and Canopy Analytics concluded that centralisation is not only possible for any firm regardless of size, but vital to its success. Sunny Junjea, CEO of Canopy Analytics, noted that operators who centralise can “cross-reference properties to find [prospects] the right home” rather than losing leads to competitors when a single community does not fit.

What centralised leasing actually looks like

In a centralised model, prospective renters interact with a leasing hub rather than an on-site agent. The hub handles:

  • Inquiry response — phone, chat, and email inquiries answered from one team, often with AI-assisted tools that maintain a 100% response rate
  • Tour scheduling — self-guided or virtual tours coordinated centrally, with automated confirmations and reminders to reduce no-shows
  • Application processing — tenant screening run through a single pipeline with consistent criteria across all properties
  • Lease execution — digital lease generation and eSignature from one platform

The on-site team’s role shifts from leasing to resident experience: handling move-ins, maintenance coordination, and renewals. As Lucretia Kumah, SVP at Bozzuto, described at the NAA session, this lets staff focus on “improving the living experience and retaining residents” rather than chasing new leads.

Who centralised leasing is for

Centralised leasing works best for operators with scale and geographic spread. Based on the patterns discussed at NAA’s Apartmentalize 2024 centralisation session and Grace Hill’s analysis of multifamily centralisation, portfolios that benefit most share three characteristics:

  • Multiple communities under one owner or manager — centralisation creates value when there are at least 3 to 5 properties to consolidate, because the shared team needs enough lead volume to justify dedicated staffing
  • High lead volume relative to on-site capacity — if on-site agents are missing calls or taking more than 24 hours to respond, a central team absorbs the overflow
  • Inconsistent leasing experience across properties — if each site runs its own process, centralisation standardises everything from screening criteria to lease terms

Operators with a single property or very small portfolios (under 50 units) typically find that the overhead of building a centralised team outweighs the efficiency gains.

How to implement centralised leasing without losing the personal touch

The most common objection operators raise is that renters want a local, personal experience. The NAA session panellists reported the opposite: renters want fast, consistent answers, regardless of who provides them. Veritas Investments found that when they showed prospects eight properties, the prospect rarely selected the initial one they inquired about, proving that a centralised team with cross-portfolio knowledge actually serves prospects better than a single-site agent.

Step 1: Audit your current leasing workflow

Map every touchpoint from first inquiry to signed lease at each property. Identify where leads drop off, where response times lag, and where the process varies between sites.

Step 2: Define what stays on-site vs. what centralises

Leasing activity centralises (inquiry response, tour scheduling, applications, screening). Resident-facing activity stays on-site (move-in, maintenance, renewals). The line is clearer than most operators expect.

Step 3: Choose your technology stack

A centralised model requires a platform that unifies listings, leads, communications, and applications in one place. The most effective setups include:

  • Listing syndication to marketplaces, so all properties publish from one dashboard
  • AI-powered inquiry response, to maintain 100% response rate across the portfolio
  • Automated tour scheduling with confirmation and reminder workflows
  • Integrated tenant screening with consistent criteria across all properties
  • Digital lease generation and eSignature

Step 4: Staff the hub and train on portfolio-wide SOP

The central team needs to know every property’s unique selling points, pricing, and policies. Create a property playbook for each community so agents can speak knowledgeably about any unit in the portfolio.

Step 5: Measure and iterate

Track the metrics that matter (see the measurement section below) and adjust staffing, scripts, and technology quarterly.

Measuring centralised leasing performance

The metrics below reflect standard leasing KPIs tracked by multifamily operators. This framework draws from benchmarks published in the NMHC research library and operational data discussed at NAA Apartmentalize 2024, where panellists reported that centralised operations drove delinquencies down by as much as 11% and pushed retention rates to around 62%, well above the industry average.
