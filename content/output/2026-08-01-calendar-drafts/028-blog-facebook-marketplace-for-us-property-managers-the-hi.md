Why does Facebook Marketplace matter more in US secondary markets?


In the major coastal metros, Zillow and Apartments.com dominate renter search. In Sun Belt and secondary markets such as Austin, Phoenix, Tampa, Charlotte, Dallas, and Atlanta, Facebook Marketplace punches far above its weight as a rental channel. It is where a large share of working renters actually search, especially after hours. According to ShowMojo, 70% of prospects search online for rental units outside normal business hours. 
For operators in oversupplied Sun Belt markets where every day on market costs real money, being slow or absent on Marketplace is not a minor gap. It is the gap.

Why does the manual Marketplace workflow break at scale for US operators?


For a small landlord with a few units, posting to Marketplace by hand works. For a US property manager running hundreds of doors across scattered Sun Belt assets, it creates three compounding problems. First, posting time per unit multiplied across turnover cycles consumes coordinator hours that should be spent on high-value leasing activities. Second, response speed suffers critically. 
The platform algorithmically rewards fast replies, and a lead that arrives at 11pm and gets a reply at 9am has usually already toured with a competitor. Third, status drift becomes inevitable. Rented units stay posted, and prospects call about apartments that are gone, wasting coordinator time and damaging your brand.
