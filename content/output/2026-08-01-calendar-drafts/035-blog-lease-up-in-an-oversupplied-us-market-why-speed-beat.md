<h1>Lease-up in an oversupplied US market: why speed beats price in the Sun Belt</h1>


<p>CBRE's Q1 2026 multifamily report (https://www.cbre.com/insights/figures/q1-2026-us-multifamily-figures) pegged US vacancy at 4.8%, down 20 basis points from Q4 2025 as absorption finally outpaced completions for the first time in three quarters (CBRE, 2026). But the relief is sharply uneven. Austin rents declined 7.2% year over year, Denver fell 4.8%, and Phoenix dropped 4.5% (Yardi Matrix, Q2 2025). If you are leasing up or filling vacancy in the Sun Belt, the instinct is to cut rent. The better lever is speed. Here is the math.</p>


<h2>The US Supply Picture in 2026</h2>


<p>The US multifamily market is absorbing the biggest supply wave in decades, with the Sun Belt bearing the heaviest impact. According to Yardi Matrix, 2026 deliveries are forecast at roughly 323,000 units, down about a third from the 477,000-unit annual average of 2023 to 2025 (Yardi Matrix, 2026). Supply is easing nationally, but in the metros that overbuilt, operators are still competing hard for every lease.</p>


<p>In Q2 2025, 39% of the 69 markets CBRE tracks had negative year-over-year rent growth, up from 26% a quarter earlier (CBRE, 2025). The biggest declines clustered in Sun Belt markets that absorbed the most new construction: Austin, Denver, Phoenix, Tampa, and Raleigh. For operators in these metros, the question is not whether the market is soft. It is what to do about it without surrendering yield.</p>


<h2>Why Price Is the Wrong First Lever</h2>


<p>In an oversupplied market, cutting rent to fill faster is the visible move. It is also the one that gives up yield permanently. A unit filled in 22 days at held rent produces more annualised income than a unit filled fast at a discount, because the discount follows the lease for its entire term while the vacancy days are a one-time cost.</p>


<p>Take a $2,200 per month unit. Compare two responses:</p>


<p><strong>Option A: Lower the rent 8% to $2,024 to fill faster.</strong> Annualised income at the lower rent: roughly $24,288.</p>


<p><strong>Option B: Hold the rent at $2,200 and compress days-on-market through operations.</strong> Annualised income: roughly $26,400.</p>


<p>The operational lever is worth roughly $2,100 per unit per year over the price lever, at the same days-on-market. For a 200-unit portfolio, that is approximately $420,000 per year of recovered yield, achieved through operations rather than pricing. In a market where asking rents are forecast to grow only 2.3% in 2026 (Yardi Matrix, 2026), there is no margin to give away.</p>


<h2>The Three Operational Levers That Move Days-on-Market</h2>


<p>The operators protecting yield in the oversupplied Sun Belt are winning on days-on-market, not on price. Three operational levers consistently compress time-to-lease without requiring concessions.</p>


<h3>1. Listing Reach</h3>


<p>Per the Rently 2026 Renter Touring Expectations Report (https://rently.com/blog/2026-renter-touring-expectations), 47% of US renters chose a less-than-ideal rental because it was easier to find and tour (Rently, 2026). In an oversupplied market, if a renter does not see your listing, reach is your ceiling. Syndication to 48+ marketplaces, including Facebook Marketplace, Apartments.com, Zillow, and local channels, ensures your units are visible where prospects are actually searching. Manual posting across these channels is operationally impractical at scale; automated syndication is the baseline.</p>


<h3>2. Response Speed</h3>


<p>ShowMojo's scheduling data shows 70% of rental inquiries arrive after business hours (ShowMojo, 2025). Research consistently indicates that 60% or more of renters go with the first operator who responds meaningfully. In an oversupplied market, the prospect who messages you at 9pm is also messaging three competitors. The operator who responds in seconds with qualification questions and tour availability captures that lead. The operator who responds the next morning does not.</p>


<p>Per the AppFolio 2025 Benchmark Report, 85% of AI adopters report lead-to-lease improvements, and the first place it shows is response speed at hours your team is not staffed (AppFolio, 2025). AI adoption in property management rose from 21% in 2024 to 34% in 2025 (AppFolio, 2025), driven primarily by the economics of capturing after-hours demand.</p>


<h3>3. Tour Follow-Through</h3>


<p>Tours with an active reconfirmation workflow attend at meaningfully higher rates than tours without one. A no-show is not just a missed tour; it is a wasted slot that could have been filled by another prospect. Recovered tour capacity at zero added lead cost is one of the highest-use operational improvements available. The Rently report makes the case directly: tour friction is one of the most common reasons a renter chooses a different listing (Rently, 2026).</p>


<p>Stack the three and operations does what a rent cut was supposed to do, without surrendering yield.</p>
