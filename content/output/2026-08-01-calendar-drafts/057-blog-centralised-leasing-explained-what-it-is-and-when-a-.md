<p>According to the AppFolio 2025 Property Management Benchmark Report (https://naahq.org/flat-rents-ai-adoption-2025-appfolio-property-management-benchmark-report-reveals-key-trends), AI adoption in property management jumped from 21% in 2024 to 34% in 2025. "Centralised leasing" is one of the fastest-rising terms in multifamily operations, and one of the most loosely defined. Here is what it actually means, when a mid-market operator needs it, and what it requires operationally.</p>

<h2>What Is Centralised Leasing?</h2>


<p>Centralised leasing consolidates the leasing function across a portfolio into one team, one system, and one consistent process, regardless of how many buildings or markets you operate. It replaces the traditional per-property model where each building has its own leasing staff, its own inbox, and its own process.</p>


<p>Instead of ten coordinators each handling their building's leads in isolation, a centralised model routes every lead through one workflow with shared visibility, consistent qualification criteria, and load-balancing across the team. The prospect gets the same response quality and speed whether they enquire about building A or building J.</p>


<p>Julia Lambert of RET Ventures, quoted by Insights by Blueprint in March 2026 (https://insightsbyblueprint.com/2026/03/30/where-ai-is-falling-short-in-multifamily-leasing-and-where-its-headed-next/), estimates that roughly 80% of inbound prospect needs fall into scenarios AI can handle. It is an industry estimate, not a measurement in leasing workflows. Centralised leasing is the operating model that makes that AI coverage consistent across an entire portfolio rather than building-by-building.</p>


<h2>Why Are Operators Moving to Centralised Leasing?</h2>


<p>The shift is driven by economics and consistency. Per-property staffing does not scale: every new building needs its own coordinator, and coverage gaps appear the moment someone is out sick, on holiday, or turns over. Centralised leasing breaks the link between portfolio growth and linear headcount growth.</p>


<p>According to Yardi's Q1 2026 national vacancy data (https://www.yardi.com/news/press-releases/rent-growth-hits-4-year-low-in-q1-2026/), Canadian vacancy reached 5.1%, while CBRE's Q1 2026 report (https://www.cbre.com/insights/figures/q1-2026-us-multifamily-figures) places US vacancy at 4.8%. In a softening market, consistency of response speed and quality across every property in the portfolio becomes a competitive advantage. A prospect who gets a response in two minutes from building A but waits four hours from building B is a prospect you lose from building B.</p>


<p>Centralised leasing also improves visibility. In a per-property model, the portfolio manager has limited insight into response times, lead quality, and conversion rates across buildings. In a centralised model, every lead, every response, and every conversion metric is visible in one dashboard, making it possible to identify underperforming properties and reallocate resources before vacancy compounds.</p>


<h2>When Does a Mid-Market Operator Actually Need Centralised Leasing?</h2>


<p>Centralised leasing is not for everyone. The signals that you are ready for it are specific and measurable. You need centralised leasing when you operate multiple buildings, especially across more than one market. When lead volume is uneven (some properties drown while others sit quiet) and your staff cannot flex across them. When response quality varies building to building and you cannot see it in one place. When you are growing and do not want to add a coordinator for every new property.</p>


<p>If you run a single building with steady volume, per-property leasing is fine. The case for centralising strengthens with every building you add to the portfolio. According to ShowMojo (https://hello.showmojo.com/blog/property-management-takeaways-and-tips/), 70% of prospects search online for rental units outside normal business hours. For multi-building operators, providing consistent after-hours coverage across every property without per-building night staff is one of the clearest economic arguments for centralisation.</p>


<h2>What Does Centralised Leasing Require Operationally?</h2>


<p>You cannot centralise leasing on per-property inboxes and spreadsheets. Centralised leasing requires a single system that ingests every lead across every channel and property, maintains one conversation thread per prospect regardless of which property they enquired about, and lets the team work from a shared queue with clear prioritisation.</p>


<p>This is exactly what a leasing automation layer provides. The AI agent (Liza, in Leasey.AI's case) handles first response and qualification across the whole portfolio with 100% response rate and 24/7 availability. Leads from 48+ marketplaces, including Facebook Marketplace, Realtor.ca, and Kijiji, flow into one queue. The AI qualifies them using your custom questions, schedules tours, sends confirmations, and routes the qualified pipeline to coordinators who work from one screen.</p>


<p>The coordinators' role shifts from chasing initial responses building by building to managing a qualified pipeline across the portfolio. That shift is the operational difference between per-property leasing and centralised leasing.</p>


<h2>How Does Centralised Leasing Work With Your Existing PMS?</h2>


<p>A common concern is that centralised leasing means replacing your PMS. It does not. Leasey.AI sits on top of existing PMS tools (Yardi, Buildium, Rent Manager) or runs standalone. Your accounting, maintenance, and owner reporting workflows remain untouched. The centralised leasing layer handles the front-office funnel: syndication, lead response, qualification, tour scheduling, confirmation, ID verification, and screening across the portfolio.</p>


<p>This is the key distinction. The PMS manages the property. The leasing automation layer fills the property. Centralising leasing does not require centralising property management. You can centralise the leasing function while keeping per-property operations, maintenance, and accounting exactly as they are.</p>
