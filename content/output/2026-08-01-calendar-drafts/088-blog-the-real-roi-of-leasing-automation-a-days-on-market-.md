<h1>The real ROI of leasing automation: a days-on-market model property managers can actually run</h1>


<p>Every day a unit sits empty in Vancouver costs more than the rent it is not collecting. In a loosening market, that day is easier to lose and harder to win back. 
CMHC's 2025 Rental Market Report (published December 2025) showed vacancy rising across every major Canadian metro: Vancouver hit 3.7% (its highest since 1988), Edmonton rose to 3.8%, Toronto reached 3%, and Calgary sat at 5% (CMHC, 2025). By Q1 2026, national vacancy had climbed to 5.1% with Calgary at 7.3% and rent growth slowing to a four-year low of 2.7% (Yardi, 2026). Every leasing software vendor claims ROI. 
Few show you the math. This is the model property managers can actually run against their own portfolio, anchored in a real Canadian lease-up.</p>


<h2>ROI in Leasing Is a Days-on-Market Calculation</h2>


<p>The return on leasing automation is not "hours saved" in the abstract. It is the vacancy days you remove, converted to dollars. Every day a unit sits vacant is pure carrying cost: you are paying debt service, taxes, insurance, and operating expense on a unit producing zero revenue. The formula is straightforward.</p>


<p><strong>ROI per unit = (vacancy days removed) x (daily rent) x (turnover events per year)</strong></p>


<p>This formula converts operational improvement into a dollar figure you can compare directly against the cost of the automation platform. The calculation works because it measures recovered revenue, not cost avoidance. A day of vacancy removed is a day of rent collected.</p>


<h2>Why This Calculation Matters More in 2026</h2>


<p>In a tighter market, the instinct is to drop rent to fill faster. In the US, multifamily vacancy sat at 4.8% in Q1 2026 (CBRE, 2026), with Sun Belt markets absorbing the heaviest supply wave. Austin rents declined 7.2% year over year, Denver fell 4.8%, and Phoenix dropped 4.5% (Yardi Matrix, Q2 2025). Roughly 323,000 US units are forecast for delivery in 2026, down from the 477,000-unit annual average of 2023 to 2025 (Yardi Matrix, 2026).</p>


<p>The better lever is to hold rent and compress days-on-market through operations. A unit filled in 22 days instead of 38 at the same rent yields more annualised income than a unit filled fast at a discount, because the discount follows the lease for its entire term while the vacancy days are a one-time cost. In a market where asking rents are forecast to grow only 2.3% nationally in the US (Yardi Matrix, 2026) and 2.7% in Canada (Yardi, 2026), there is no margin to give away.</p>

Syndication to 48+ marketplaces, including Facebook Marketplace, Kijiji, Apartments.com, Zillow, and Realtor.ca, ensures your units are visible where prospects are actually searching. Manual posting across these channels is operationally impractical at portfolio scale.</p>

<h2>How to Run the ROI Model on Your Portfolio</h2>


<p>The model is straightforward and every property manager has the data to run it. Four steps:</p>


<p><strong>1. Pull your current average days-on-market by property.</strong> Most PMS platforms track this. If yours does not, estimate from your last 12 months of lease start dates versus unit availability dates.</p>


<p><strong>2. Estimate the compression from the three levers.</strong> A 10 to 18 day improvement is a realistic range depending on your starting baseline. Portfolios with slower current response times and fewer syndication channels see larger improvements.</p>


<p><strong>3. Multiply (days removed) x (daily rent) x (annual turnover) per unit, then sum across the portfolio.</strong> This gives you the total recovered yield in dollars.</p>


<p><strong>4. Compare that recovered yield against the cost of the automation layer.</strong> At approximately $1.50 to $5 per door per month, or roughly $499 per month (Leasey.AI, 2026), the recovered yield is typically several multiples of the platform cost for mid-market operators.</p>
